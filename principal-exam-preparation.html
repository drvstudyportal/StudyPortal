A preparation plan that fits a teaching job

Most candidates for this exam are already teaching full time. The plan below is ordered by return on effort, not by how the syllabus is printed.

  1. 1Build the administration base firstAdministration, finance and service rules are learnable, high-scoring and rarely part of a teacher's day-to-day work — which makes them the fastest source of marks for most candidates. Serving teachers usually arrive strong on pedagogy and weak here, so front-loading this section is what changes a score most quickly.
  2. 2Layer on education perspectives and leadershipNEP, RTE, curriculum design, school governance and leadership theory. Because you already work in a school, connect every concept to a real situation you have handled — that is also what the interview will probe.
  3. 3Keep general awareness and language ticking overThese reward consistency rather than intensity. Short daily contact beats long weekend sessions.
  4. 4Move to full-length mocks under exam conditionsSectional practice tells you what you know; full-length timed mocks tell you whether you can deliver it in the time available. Analysing a mock properly takes longer than sitting it.
  5. 5Prepare the interview from the day you applyThe interview is a substantial part of final selection, and it typically explores your vision for a school — often through a school development plan and a question-and-answer round. Candidates who start this only after clearing the written stage are already behind.
